## The two conventions that look right and are not

Both are documented at length in RESEARCH.md; both fail silently in self-consistent
tests and only fail loudly against a real client. Read Pitfall 1 and Pitfall 2 before
writing a line of this plan.

1. **Key direction is the mirror opposite of tls-crypt's.** `internal/tlscrypt.NewWrapper`
   assigns `server: encrypt=keys[0], decrypt=keys[1]`. The data channel assigns
   `server: encrypt=keys[1], decrypt=keys[0]`. Plan 02-01's `keyderiv.Key2.ServerSlots()`
   already encodes the correct mapping — consume it; do not re-derive slot indices here,
   and do not pattern-match against `tlscrypt`'s constructor.
2. **The AEAD tag precedes the ciphertext on the wire.** Go's `Seal` appends the tag;
   OpenVPN puts it first. Both directions need an explicit reorder.

A round-trip test built only from this package's own `Seal` and `Open` will pass with
either mistake present. The gates that catch them are the live-client task below and
plan 02-04's golden vectors.

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
