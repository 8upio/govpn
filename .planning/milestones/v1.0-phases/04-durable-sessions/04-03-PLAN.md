<objective>
Prove it against the real thing. A pinned, unmodified OpenVPN 2.6 client — configured with a
shortened `reneg-sec` and `explicit-exit-notify` — renegotiates repeatedly against the library
while HTTP and UDP traffic keeps flowing through the tunnel, and then leaves cleanly, with the
server observing the close immediately rather than after a timeout.

Phase goal (ROADMAP, verbatim): *A connected client stays usable for hours and leaves cleanly —
key renegotiation does not break traffic, and dead sessions do not accumulate.*

Purpose: ROADMAP success criteria 1 and 2 for this phase. The fast tier proves the mechanics
against a synthetic client; only a real client proves the wire behavior.
Output: two new interop scenarios sharing one compose overlay, wired into the existing scenario
table and probe framework from plan 03-06.
</objective>

<user_decisions>
Decisions this plan implements (from `04-CONTEXT.md`):

- **D-23** — New interop scenario with the renegotiation interval shortened to ~20s on both sides;
  assert HTTP and UDP probes succeed before, during and after at least two rollovers; the client log
  shows the renegotiations and zero reconnects.
- **D-21** (live half) — The client's explicit-exit-notify ends its session immediately, observed by
  the server rather than waited out.
- **D-19** (live half) — `Config.RenegSec` exists precisely so the harness can shorten it;
  `reneg-sec` is not pushed, so the client config must set its own.

RESEARCH Pitfall 3 is the reason this plan exists as its own unit: the shipped `client.conf` sets
neither `explicit-exit-notify` nor `reneg-sec`, so both of this phase's client-facing success
criteria are untestable against the real client until it does.
</user_decisions>
